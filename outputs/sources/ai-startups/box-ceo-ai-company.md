# $4B Box CEO: This Is The Best Time To Build An AI Company

## Summary

- Aaron Levy (CEO of Box, $4B enterprise content management company) argues there is a 3-year window of compressed opportunity to build AI-native businesses
- Agents still require humans at process boundaries — accountability cannot fully transfer to software
- The "accountability gap": you can't fire an agent, demote it, or hold it legally responsible — this limits full autonomy deployment in enterprise settings
- "Internet of agents": Outshift by Cisco and agency.org are building infrastructure for agents to discover and transact with other agents
- Levy is bullish overall but notes "prompt fatigue" as a real user experience risk for agentic UIs

## Key Insights

- **3-year window is the strategic claim.** Levy argues that the combination of accessible AI APIs, enterprise willingness to experiment, and unoccupied market positions creates a compressed window before incumbents (including Box itself) own the space. Build now.
- **Accountability gap is the hard limit on autonomy.** Humans remain in the loop not because AI can't execute tasks, but because enterprises need someone to hold responsible when things go wrong. You can't fire software. This is a cultural/legal constraint, not a technical one.
- **Internet of agents as the next infrastructure layer.** Agency.org and Outshift (Cisco's venture unit) are building discovery and transaction protocols for agent-to-agent interaction — analogous to how APIs enabled service-to-service calls. If this becomes infrastructure, it enables agent orchestration at internet scale.
- **Prompt fatigue is a UX risk.** As agentic interfaces proliferate, users face cognitive load from constant prompt-and-respond cycles. Levy sees this as a design problem for the field: how do you build agentic UIs that don't exhaust users?
- **AI augments, doesn't replace (at enterprise scale).** For regulated industries with accountability requirements, Levy expects AI to amplify human workers rather than eliminate roles. The inflection point is when legal/regulatory frameworks catch up.
