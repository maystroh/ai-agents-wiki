## ❓ Open Questions

1. **Which "inner/outer harness" split is the useful one — vendor-vs-user, or within-turn-vs-across-session?** Contradiction 1 is a symptom of a real conceptual question with different implications for what to build in-house vs. rely on the vendor for.

2. **When does harness investment become net-negative?** The wiki preaches "build to delete" and "thin harness" while documenting ever-more-sophisticated infrastructure (brain/hands decoupling, event-log durability, DSL interpreters, vault memory). No framework separates durable-investment harness layers (security, observability, audit) from those the model will absorb.

3. **How many agents can one human actually supervise?** Sources span three orders of magnitude: "hundreds to thousands" (Boris Cherny) vs. "4–5 sessions" as the human ceiling (Sid Boudesaria) vs. "3–6 parallel sessions" (Eugene Yan). Nobody in the wiki reconciles these, and the binding constraint — human review bandwidth — has no model. This is exactly the question the missing Parallel Agent Workflows page would need to answer.

4. **How should memory quality itself be evaluated?** `evals-and-graders.md` covers task-output evaluation thoroughly, but Dreaming's memory-store diff has no eval framework for coverage, precision, or freshness. The 97%/6× figures are outcome metrics without baselines (Data Gap 8), not diagnostics.

5. **Can autoresearch close the eval-saturation loop without humans?** The wiki states plainly that static benchmarks saturate at autoresearch speeds and eval suites must "evolve," but no source describes a mechanism for *autonomously* evolving one. If humans must keep hand-updating evals, autoresearch stays human-bound at exactly the layer it claims to automate — an unexamined dependency in Jack Clark's 2028 forecast.
